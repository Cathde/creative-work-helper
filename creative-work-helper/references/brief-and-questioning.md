# Brief and Questioning

## Minimum viable brief

Only clarify dimensions that can materially alter the work:

1. Why: problem, goal, desired audience change or action.
2. Who: primary audience, context, prior knowledge, accessibility or cultural needs.
3. Where: channel, physical/digital environment, discovery and consumption context.
4. What: deliverable, scope, variants, and whether format should remain Open.
5. How it should feel: tone, emotional response, visual/narrative language, references, avoids.
6. Constraints: time, budget, people, assets, technology, approvals, policy, rights, reputation.
7. Success: evidence, decision maker, and threshold for “good enough.”

## Question priority

Ask now when an answer could change the strategic direction, audience, format family, production feasibility, or risk profile. Defer when an answer only affects details that can be selected after a direction is chosen.

Good questions explain why the answer matters and provide useful contrast:

> Is the main job to make unfamiliar audiences understand the topic, or to motivate people who already understand it to participate? The first favors clarity and demonstration; the second favors identity and social proof.

When the user is unsure, offer options, keep the field Open, or adopt a Tentative hypothesis. Never force certainty.

## Conflict handling

Name conflicts neutrally: “The premium tone and highly participatory format may pull in different directions.” Then offer choices: prioritize one, find a bridge, or explore both. Do not silently resolve meaningful tension.

## Output contract

Ask about audience, purpose, format, length, level of detail, variants, file/channel constraints, deadline, and review context only when relevant. If unspecified, propose a compact default based on work type and invite edits.
